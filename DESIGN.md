# Design System: RelaxJer trip page

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Sky Lanterns on a Working String"** _(name inferred from the direction contract's thesis)_

Each trip day is one sky-lantern panel. The day's goal is brush-inked on lantern paper in that day's colour, and the working plan (times, transport, budget, rain rule) is printed plainly underneath on white paper, threaded on a lantern string. The world has two layers. The lantern layer (brush lettering, lantern paper, seal stamps, strings) is atmosphere and identity. The operated layer (system sans, large tabular numbers, 44 px+ targets, plain rows) is what the group actually uses on a phone, often with seniors, in bright sunlight or a dark hotel room. The operated layer never borrows the lantern layer's ornament.

The page shows a glance layer and hides the rest ([ADR-20261128-progressive-disclosure](memory-bank/standards/decisions/ADR-20261128-progressive-disclosure.md)). The group opens it tens of times a day for one answer each time, so what most opens need stays open, one line each: Now and Next, tonight's hotel, the week, a day's card and its string of stops. Everything else is one tap away, through two kinds of disclosure: a list you scan folds under a big heading with its count and a chevron; a single part you open and close is a tile, a big icon over a short word, that opens its sheet. Words stay under every icon; sentences move into the sheets. One phone-first column, generous type (a 17 px body that follows the phone's text-size setting), and flat paper-grey containers. By day it is white paper. In dark mode it is a night sky, and each lantern glows from inside with a warm light rising from its base. The first viewport is a translucent sticky header (brush brand, search, the currency and language switches) over Today: the Now/Next card with a monumental countdown, then the week as a list of small lanterns. On a phone and a tablet a glass bottom bar (Today, Days, Map, More) stands at the foot of every view, the way around the trip; a desktop has the week rail instead.

It rejects the travel-app default: stock hero photo, teal accent, decorative feature cards. It also rejects the opposite failure it once had: a page that prints every sentence the planner wrote, so the answer sits under a wall of words.

**Key Characteristics:**

- One lantern colour per trip day, cycling through seven, carried by the Days row, the bottom bar's lit Days, the lantern, the string, the knots, the sticky day bar and the map pins.
- Brush lettering for the brand, the day's wish and the seal glyph only; system sans for everything operated.
- A lantern string runs down every schedule; each stop sits on it as a one-line row, its kind (a meal, a ride, a walk, a sight, the hotel) drawn as its knot; fixed anchors are red diamond knots with a brush-lettered seal.
- Two disclosures, one per kind of content: folds for lists, tiles for single parts. Nothing reached only sideways.
- Flat paper-grey containers; shadows only on things that float and on lanterns.
- Motion is short, press-scale feedback and state fades; jumps land instantly and a ring holds on the target.

## Colors

White paper and near-black ink carry the operated layer; seven saturated lantern papers carry the days; one seal red marks what does not move.

### Primary

- **Lantern Papers** (`lantern-1` to `lantern-7`: Firecracker Red, Temple Blue, Persimmon, Jade, Plum, Peach Blossom, Imperial Gold): one per trip day, assigned in order and cycling after seven. The engine writes the day's pair into `--c` / `--ci` on each day's elements, so a day's colour is its identity everywhere it appears. Each paper has its own ink (`-ink`): white on the four deep papers, a near-black of the same hue on Persimmon, Peach Blossom and Gold. All seven pairs reach at least 4.6:1 on flat paper.

### Secondary

- **Seal Red** (`seal`; `night-seal` in dark): the vermilion of a name stamp. It marks fixed-time anchors (the diamond knot, the seal stamp and its "fixed" label). Nothing else.

### Tertiary

- **Link Blue** (`link`) and **Focus Blue** (`focus`): links, link-buttons and the 3 px focus ring.
- **Highlighter** (`hl`, `hl-now`): search hits, the current hit, the landing flash and the yellow callout tint. Same value in both themes.
- **Star** (`star`, `night-star`): the rating star and the Rating button's icon, a darker gold by day so the icon reaches 3:1 on paper-2.
- **Mine** (`mine-ink`, `night-mine-ink`): "Add to plan" and the "Added" tag on a stop the group added, at 4.5:1 in both themes.
- **Status inks** (`good`, `warn`, `bad`): go / wait / skip decisions, checked items, the progress fill and a split plan's "picked" badge, always as a 14–18 % tint behind a full-strength icon.
- **Nearby icons** (fixed values, same in both themes, icons only): teal `#0f8a7e` for drinks, plum `#a0527e` for rest spots, slate blue `#4a6fa5` for toilets, Refill Azure `#0a84c1` for water refill (a drop: 4.1:1 on paper, 4.6:1 on night paper; its map pin is the drop in white outline, its tap tag a 14 % tint with `#075f8c` words, `#8fd0f5` at night). The pill's text stays `ink`, and each kind has its own shape, so the colour is never the only cue.

### Neutral

- **Day Paper** (`paper`): page ground, hanging cards, tags, the Back pill.
- **Fog Grey** (`paper-2`): the default container: Now/Next card, week rows, link-buttons, decision rows; and the ground behind the grouped lists' cards.
- **Grouped lists** (`--gl-ground`, `--gl-card`, `--gl-tile`, `--gl-press`, `--gl-sep`, `--gl-end`, `--gl-r`, `--gl-pad`; [ADR-20261124-grouped-lists](memory-bank/standards/decisions/ADR-20261124-grouped-lists.md)): the rail, Days, More and the Sections sheet. By day, white cards (`paper`) on Fog Grey (`paper-2`); at night, cards one step lighter (`night-paper-2`) on the page's own dark (`night-paper`). The icon tile is the card mixed 8 % toward ink (12 % at night); a pressed row is Mist Grey by day and the card mixed 16 % toward ink at night; the hairline between rows is `rule`; a row's end mark (`--gl-end`) is Ink 3 by day and Ink 2 at night, so › still reads at night in the squint test; the card's corners follow the world's button radius (`--r-btn`, 14 px in the default world).
- **Mist Grey** (`paper-3`): pressed states, the search field, the segmented control's track, image placeholders.
- **Ink** (`ink`), **Ink 2** (`ink-2`), **Ink 3** (`ink-3`): text, secondary text, tertiary labels. Ink 3 still clears 6.3:1 on Fog Grey.
- **Rule** (`rule`): 1 px hairlines between rows and around hanging cards. It is also the sticky bar's stripe colour when no day is in view.
- **Glass** (`glass`, `glass-edge`) and **Scrim** (`scrim`): the translucent header and day bar, their 1 px underline, and the dialog backdrop.
- **Scroll thumb** (`scroll-thumb`, `scroll-thumb-on` under the pointer): with a mouse or trackpad, scrollbars drawn as macOS draws them, a thin rounded thumb in the ink at 45% (62% under the pointer, 70% with more contrast asked for) with no track, inset from the edge; Firefox gets the standard thin, trackless bar. Phones keep their own overlay scrollbar (a styled one takes layout space in WebKit). Rows that hide their scrollbar keep hiding it.
- **Night Sky** (`night-*`): the dark theme's paper, ink, rule, glass and scrim, a blue-black ground (`night-paper`) with cool grey inks.

### Data colours (from the region, not the brand)

- **Metro line colours** (`metro-*`) are the transit operator's own line colours, currently the Taipei Metro set in the stylesheet, and transit-planner lines take their colour from the region's data. They identify a line, not a mood. A new destination brings its own set; they are never reused as accents.

### Named Rules

**The One Lantern Per Day Rule.** A lantern colour means "this day" and nothing else. Every element that belongs to a day takes that day's `--c` / `--ci` pair; nothing that belongs to no day uses a lantern colour.

**The Seal Means Fixed Rule.** Seal red is reserved for anchors that never move (fixed times) and the today marker. A seal on something that can shift is a lie.

**The Data Colour Rule.** Line colours come from the region and are data. Don't restyle them to match the brand, and don't use them as decoration.

## Typography

**Display Font:** the trip's brush face, `TRIP.brushFont` (default Ma Shan Zheng), with STKaiti / KaiTi / Kaiti SC / BiauKai as fallbacks. The build subsets it through Google Fonts to only the glyphs the page letters (the brand, each day's wish, the principle's wish and the seal glyph) and puts that subset in the page, so the lettering never waits on the network ([ADR-20261002-embed-brush-font](memory-bank/standards/decisions/ADR-20261002-embed-brush-font.md)).
**Body Font:** the phone's system sans (`-apple-system`, PingFang SC, Noto Sans SC, Segoe UI, Roboto…), with tabular numerals on by default.
**Native Font:** text marked `lang="ja"` (the native layer's Japanese, the driver card in Japan) takes the system Japanese sans (`-apple-system`, Hiragino Sans, Hiragino Kaku Gothic ProN, Noto Sans JP, Yu Gothic, Meiryo), so kanji shared with Chinese draw the Japanese way. Same size and weight as the text around it.

**Character:** a hand-inked calligraphic voice for names and wishes, set against a heavy, plain system sans that reads at arm's length in sunlight. Weights sit high (650–800) for anything a person has to act on.

### Hierarchy

- **Display, countdown** (800, 4.25rem, 0.95, −0.04em): the Now/Next countdown number only. Monumental on purpose.
- **Display, driver card** (800, 2.25rem, 1.2): the destination name in the "show the driver" sheet, read by someone else across a car.
- **Brush, wish** (400, 2.625rem; 3.25rem from 720 px; 2.125rem below 600 px; 1.12): the day's goal on its lantern.
- **Brush, brand** (400, 1.625rem, 1): the trip name in the header.
- **Headline** (800, 1.75rem, −0.02em): section titles.
- **Title** (800, 1.4375rem, −0.015em): the day's route title on its lantern.
- **Subhead** (750, 1.1875rem, −0.01em): sub-sections inside a day (schedule, route, rain plan).
- **Time, fixed** (750, 1.3125rem, −0.02em): the time of a fixed anchor, larger than every other time on the string.
- **Body** (400, 1.0625rem, 1.55): running text and stop names (at 700). Section ledes cap at 38rem.
- **Body small** (600–750, 0.9375rem): notes, chips, buttons, times, meta. This is the most used size in the system.
- **Label** (600, 0.8125rem): compact labels only: header dates, the bottom bar's labels, chart axes, table heads, search counts.

### Named Rules

**The Brush Is For Names Rule.** The brush face letters the brand, the day's wish and the seal glyph. Never a time, price, instruction or button.

**The Local Words Wear Their Own Script Rule.** Words in the destination's language carry their `lang`, never the page's, so the phone picks the local glyphs (ADR-20261117-native-layer).

**The Phone Decides The Size Rule.** Type is set in rem on a root that takes `-apple-system-body` where supported (never smaller than 1rem), so the phone's text-size setting scales the whole page. Don't pin text in px.

## Layout

One phone-first column, `--col` (46rem) wide, centred, with a 16 px gutter. Only the header spans wider (with the rail, from 1024 px). Sections open with 36 px above them; each day starts 40 px below the previous one; blocks inside a day sit 22 px apart (16 px on phones). Internal gaps step through 4 / 6 / 8 / 10 / 12 / 14 px. The main column reserves 88 px at the bottom so the last row scrolls clear of the floating buttons; below 1024 px each view's foot keeps `--chrome-bottom` (the bottom bar plus the home bar) and 76 px more, for the Back pill above the bar.

The header is sticky and translucent (20 px blur, 180 % saturation) and measures its own height into `--bar-h`. Below it, each day has a sticky 40 px day bar (the day's swatch, date and route) that fades in when that day's lantern scrolls under the header. Anchored targets keep a scroll margin of `--bar-h` + 12–56 px so a jump never lands under the header.

A schedule row is a three-column grid: the start time (4.25rem, right-aligned), the 26 px string with the stop's kind on it, then the stop's one line and its chevron. Now/Next rows and fact rows are two-column key/value grids (5.25rem and 6.5rem keys). On a trip day the Now/Next card's two actions (Open today, Go to next stop) come right under the rows and share one row, a long label wrapping inside its 48 px button, so both sit on an iPhone's first screen (390 × 664); the location ask follows them (a repeated hint: in full the first time, one row after). Tiles sit in a grid of four equal columns on a phone, 8 px apart, five when there are exactly five (still 8 px apart, 2 px side padding), so no tile is left alone on a row; from 600 px the columns split the tiles into even rows, eight at most, none wider than 6.5rem (`--cw`, set with the tiles). Never a sideways row.

**The layers, top to bottom.** Today: the band (dates with a calendar mark, Day N), Now/Next (or the countdown), tonight's hotel card, the week (one row a day: its date, the seal on a day with a fixed time, its short name, its forecast while inside it, →), the "Bookings and info" tiles, then the folds (Trip facts, Backups). A day: the band (date, wish, weather), the card (picture, date big, wish, title, one line of icon facts, ⋯), the day's tiles under a Label-size heading with their count, then the schedule, the photos and the pager.

**Phone compaction (below 600 px, checked at 390 px):** secondary button labels collapse to icons and stay available to screen readers; the brush wish drops to 2.125rem; blocks tighten to 16 px and hanging cards to 12 px padding; the bottom bar takes the Sections button's place (below 1024 px). From 440 px down the header drops the trip dates, and on the one opening Install shows beside the currency button (its first time on this phone), Install keeps its word but drops its icon and the brush brand steps down to 1.25rem, so a six-character brand still fits at 390 px; every later opening the header holds only the brand, search, the currency and the language. From 720 px up the wish grows to 3.25rem, photos run three to a row, travel methods go two-up and tiers three-up. A method's three facts sit side by side at every width and never split a word or a number range ("60–75 min"): a cell takes the room its longest one needs, and wraps to a full row of its own only when the three can't fit.

### Named Rules

**The One Column Rule.** Everything the group reads lives in the 46rem column. Wider screens get more margin, not more columns, except for grids of equals (methods, tiers, photos, tiles).

**The Glance Layer Rule.** Open by default only what most opens need: Now and Next, tonight's hotel, the week, a day's card and its string of stops, each one line (two at most). Everything else waits behind a fold or a tile. A new thing on the page starts hidden unless it belongs to that set.

**The Two Disclosures Rule.** A list you scan is a fold (a native `details`: big heading, count, a chevron that turns, opened in place, found by search). A single part you open and close is a tile that opens its sheet. Don't make a third kind, and never put tiles in a sideways scroller.

**The Full Once Rule.** A hint the page repeats (the location ask, the header's Install) shows in full the first time this phone sees it: what it does, why, the action. Every later opening it is one row (an icon, a few words, the action, ✕; Install: a row in More). Its ✕ puts it away for good on this phone (`store` `hints`, never synced), and the feature stays where it lives: Location and Install in More's settings. A state that needs the group to act now (running late, a clash, a blocked device) is not a hint and never shrinks.

## Elevation & Depth

Hybrid. Content is flat and tonal: Fog Grey and Mist Grey containers on white paper, separated by hairline rules, with no shadow at rest. Shadows belong to two things only: surfaces that float above the page (header glass, results sheet, toast, Back pill, sections button, dialogs) and the lanterns themselves, whose shadow is tinted with their own colour so they read as lit paper, not cards. In dark mode the dark-ink lanterns (3, 6, 7) switch from a flat fill to a radial glow rising from below the base (the paper mixed 38 % toward warm `#fff4c9`); the white-ink ones (1, 2, 4, 5) keep their flat fill, because lightening the paper under white ink took text below 4.5:1. Every lantern's shadow blooms in its own hue.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Glass underline** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 0 var(--glass-edge)`): the sticky header and day bar.
- **Lantern, day** (`box-shadow: 0 10px 24px -14px color-mix(in srgb, var(--c) 80%, #000)`): every lantern panel in light mode.
- **Lantern, night** (`box-shadow: 0 16px 40px -18px color-mix(in srgb, var(--c) 90%, transparent)`): the lantern's glow in dark mode.
- **Float** (`box-shadow: 0 10px 28px -10px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.45)`): the Back pill (0.55 on a desktop's sections button), the map's side panel; cast upward (`0 -10px …`) on the map's list sheet. The bottom bar is glass, like the header, with a hairline on top (`0 -1px 0 var(--glass-edge)`).
- **Toast** (`box-shadow: 0 10px 30px -10px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.4)`).
- **Sheet** (`box-shadow: 0 30px 80px -20px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.5)`): dialogs over the scrim.

### Named Rules

**The Glow From Inside Rule.** At night the page stays flat and dark; only lanterns give off light. Don't add glows to anything else.

**The Float Earns A Shadow Rule.** A shadow means "this is above the page and will move". Content rows and containers stay flat.

## Shapes

Softly rounded paper. Containers use 12 px (`md`) and the two hero surfaces (Now/Next card and lanterns) use 18 px (`lg`). Controls sit between them at 8–12 px, with 10 px as the common control radius. Floating buttons use 16 px, dialogs 20 px. Full rounding is used only for dots, the live-location marker and metro line badges.

The lantern has three details. It has a paper-fibre texture (fractal noise in soft-light at 22 % opacity) and two bamboo-ring hairlines inset 9 px from its top and bottom. Its bottom also carries a divider that picks up the day ink at 22 %. The schedule string is a 2 px line in the day colour at 55 %. Ordinary stops are 12 px rings (paper fill, 2.5 px day-colour border). A fixed anchor is a solid seal-red square rotated 45° into a diamond. The current stop is a filled knot with a 5 px halo. The seal stamp is a 20 px, 3 px-radius red square holding one brush glyph, tilted −4°. Optional items hang below on a short 2 px string. One the trip suggests at a stop shows under it as a dashed chip in a "Nearby options" row (the same row and chip as the nearby wishlist, with the list icon), and opens its card in the sheet. A split day (part of the group on its own plan) forks at a dashed knot in the day colour: the plan's name sits on the string like a stop, its fold-out spans the full width on phones, and the stop where they rejoin carries a quiet "rejoins here" line. A busy button turns its icon and says what it is doing; its result shows for a moment (a tick on `good`, or the reason on a `warn` tint) before it resets. The rain plan hangs from its lantern as a dashed 1.5 px border in the day colour.

### Named Rules

**The Word Under Every Icon Rule.** A tile, a labelled icon button and a fact keep their words at the Label size or larger, in both languages, at every width; English words wrap to a second line rather than shrink or cut. Seniors read the word, not the glyph.

**The Two Lines Rule.** A collapsed row (a stop, a day in Days, a fixed anchor) is one line, two at most: its name, then for a fixed time the seal and its range, or a note the group must act on.

**The Skipped Stays Visible Rule.** A skipped stop leaves the string but never the page: it folds into one line on the string with its count, greyed and struck through inside, one tap from Put back. Nothing the group skips is moved, re-timed or removed by itself.

**The Must-Do Note Rule.** A stop's note opens with the stop, except a note that is something the group must do (wear a hat, book ahead, be on time): marked `todo` in the data, it stays in sight as a short second line with a flag. Advice and colour stay inside.

**The One Number Rule.** On the glance layer a cost is one number, each person's share in the currency the phone reads, marked "about" and "estimate" where it is one. Every other form of it (the range, the group's figure, the other currency, the source) is in the sheet.

### Toast with Undo

A removal or reset shows the toast with an Undo chip for 8 seconds, on every page. The chip is 44 px tall with a 9 px radius, a paper tint at 18 % on the ink toast, and a paper focus ring. The toast lifts above the Sections button while the chip is there, and keeps its width to its content.

### Motion

Easing is `--ease-out` for anything that moves (`--ease-in-out` only for a loop that swings both ways, such as a destination world's candle); the landing ring is the exception, holding and then fading linearly. Presses take effect at once on touch-down and ease back over 160 ms on release. Tab colours, toasts, the Back pill, the sections button and the day bar take 200 ms. The now stripe takes 240 ms and dialogs 220 ms. The landing flash fades out over 900 ms, and the landing ring (3 px, the day colour mixed 55 % with ink, so a gold day still reaches 3:1) holds for 1.5 s, then fades over 0.8 s. A place picked on the map lands with one halo: a 3 px ink ring the size of its disc grows to 2.6× while it fades, over 1.6 s on `--ease-out`; the ringed disc stays. A tab jump lands instantly, and the ring marks the target's heading or lantern so the reader sees where they arrived. A new view's first three blocks slide 16 px in from the side it comes from (a later day from the right, an earlier one from the left) and fade in over 220 ms, 30 ms apart (`--ease-out`). A part sheet rises from the bottom over 420 ms on `--ease-drawer` (`cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)`, the iOS drawer curve); dragged by its head it follows the finger (rubber-banding upward), closes when the drag would carry it past 45 % of its height or flicks faster than 900 px/s, and otherwise springs back (response 0.3 s, damping 0.82), carrying the finger's speed. The desktop panel slides 40 px in from the right, with no drag. Reduced motion drops all transitions to 1 ms, keeps only opacity fades on the toast, results, dialogs and a new view (160 ms), keeps the landing ring as a static outline for the same 2.4 s, removes the full-screen map entrance and the picked place's halo (its ringed disc is the static cue), and freezes the location pulse.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** make every control signal what it does before and after a tap, per `memory-bank/standards/patterns/affordances.md` (targets, states, feedback, scroll and disclosure cues).
- **Do** keep the glance layer open and one line deep, and put the rest behind a fold (lists) or a tile (single parts); move sentences into the sheet, keep the word under every icon.
- **Do** show money on the glance layer as one number, each person's share in the currency the phone reads, marked as about (and as an estimate where the plan says so); the range and the group's figure live in the sheet.
- **Do** give every day-owned element the day's `--c` / `--ci` pair, and nothing else a lantern colour.
- **Do** keep every tap target at 44 px or more, and the main floating actions at 52 px.
- **Do** mark every number that is not the plan's own as an estimate: a "(估)" / "≈" in text, a dashed 22 % tint on charts.
- **Do** show a decision's rule with go / wait / skip marks in `good` / `warn` / `bad` tints, and the fallback beside it.
- **Do** keep lantern ink at 4.5:1 or better against the paper it actually sits on, in both themes, including the lit base of a night lantern.
- **Do** land jumps instantly and ring the target for 1.5 s, and keep the Back pill available to undo the jump.
- **Do** set type in rem so the phone's text-size setting and `-apple-system-body` scale it.
- **Do** honour `prefers-reduced-motion`, `prefers-reduced-transparency` (glass becomes solid paper) and `prefers-contrast: more` (glass becomes solid paper with a 1 px Ink 3 (`ink-3`) edge, and link buttons and tabs get the same edge).

### Don't:

- **Don't** open with a stock hero photo, use a teal accent, or build decorative feature cards. Tiles are controls: each opens its own sheet and names it.
- **Don't** print a sentence on the glance layer that a tap could show, a figure four ways, or an icon without its word (except the conventional glyphs, affordances rule 9).
- **Don't** put tiles or folds in a sideways scroller.
- **Don't** set times, prices, instructions or buttons in the brush face.
- **Don't** use seal red for anything that can move; it marks fixed anchors and today only.
- **Don't** restyle or reuse metro line colours; they belong to the region's data.
- **Don't** add shadows to content rows or containers; shadows are for floating surfaces and lanterns.
- **Don't** add glows to anything but the lanterns at night.
- **Don't** add a second content column for reading; wide screens get margin.
- **Don't** describe people on the page by family relationship; use "the group" or names.
- **Don't** assume the lantern world is the only one: a destination pack may dress the page in its own world (paper, day colours, ornament, icon) while the operated layer (type scale, tap targets, fixed-time meaning, states, layout) stays exactly as above. Everything in this file so far is the default world; each destination world below says only what it changes ([ADR-20261117-destination-themes](memory-bank/standards/decisions/ADR-20261117-destination-themes.md)).

## Destination worlds

A destination pack may name a world (`export const theme = '<name>'`, in `destinations/<cc>/regions/<city>/pack.mjs` or the country's pack). The build then adds `engine/src/themes/<name>.css` after the stylesheet and sets `data-world="<name>"` on `<html>`; a trip whose pack names none carries none of it, and its page is the default world above, unchanged. A world:

- re-tints tokens and re-draws ornament only, every rule under `:root[data-world='<name>']`;
- keeps the operated layer: the type scale, 44 px targets, layout, the Now/Next card's rows, the seal's meaning (fixed, nothing else), focus rings and states;
- keeps the engine's class names (`.lantern`, `.lantern-mark`, `.stop-knot`): they are the default world's names, kept so its page stays byte-for-byte the same. A world's documentation names things by what they show in that world, never by those class names;
- gives each day an activity mark (`DAYS[i].motif`, chosen by the planner, never read from a day's text) in the week list and on the day's card;
- draws the home-screen and tab icons when the trip has no `img/icon/` (`<name>.mjs` `iconPaint`, rendered at build by `engine/icons.mjs`, each PNG carrying its provenance), with the trip's leading activity drawn beside the emblem when the world can draw it (`iconMarks`; Snow Light Path draws a rider in a red jacket carving through powder, the spray fanning up behind, lit from behind by the candle's glow, with the candle small in the drift ahead; the sky carries six-armed snow crystals either way), and sets the browser bar's colour to its own paper.

**The sky** ([ADR-20261122-day-views](memory-bank/standards/decisions/ADR-20261122-day-views.md); the world's `sky` is `'live'`, and a trip picks another with `TRIP.sky`: `'globe'`, `'pile'` or `'off'`): every view opens under a band of sky, full bleed, holding its title; on a day, the date, the wish and the weather line (the forecast in words, low and high, the chance of precipitation, what to wear: `.wx-line`, `.wx-wear`). The band's colour is the day's weather (`data-wx`: `heavy`, `light`, `shower`, `clear`, `night`), each a set of `wx-<kind>-*` tokens, with the day colour blended in at `sky-band-tint`; the colours move between views over 0.7 s (registered `@property` colours). Snow is drawn on a canvas (`24-sky.js`, `core/sky.mjs`), never DOM nodes: at most 110 flakes on a phone and 240 on a desktop, dropping to every other frame when a frame's work passes 9 ms, and nothing at all while the page is hidden, the snow is paused or the phone asks for less motion.

- **Live weather:** snow falls by itself, as the day's weather says: heavy (0.85 flakes per 1000 px², 1.4–3.8 px, 50–105 px/s, a 26 px/s wind), light (0.24; 1.1–2.5 px; 20–42 px/s), showers (0.55, in gusts), clear (a few slow glints), clear night (stars, no snow). A day with no forecast falls as light snow.
- **Snow globe:** the snow rests in the band's foot until Shake (摇一摇) or a sideways swipe on the band swirls it; the swirl settles in about 5 s (energy halves every 1.5 s). Nothing reads the phone's motion sensors.
- **Snow piles:** snow settles on the band's drift (`snow-drift-rim`); on a desktop on the snowy ground and on the rail's and the view's cards (a cap of `snow-cap` with a `snow-cap-rim` edge).
- **Where it falls:** on a phone and a tablet the sky fills the header and the band; the band's words sit on their own layer above the canvas, so the snow falls behind them. From 1024 px the sky is behind the whole page (`.sky-full`), each view is a paper card on it, and the snow falls in the margins. The pause (`#skyBtn`, 44 px, in a desktop's header beside search; below 1024 px a row in the bottom bar's More, `[data-sky-toggle]`, two taps from any view: [ADR-20261124-bottom-app-bar](memory-bank/standards/decisions/ADR-20261124-bottom-app-bar.md)) is remembered with `store` (`skyPaused`), and is hidden with less motion asked for, when the sky is still anyway. A phone header too narrow for the whole brand shows the trip's short name (`TRIP.brandShort`). Under a sky, a phone's Today drops the scene above Now and Next (the band is its sky) and keeps the trip's name for screen readers, so Now and Next keep the first screen. With `'off'` the page keeps the still backdrop below.

**Illustrations** (`engine/src/themes/snowlight-art/`, hand-drawn SVG, MIT): one wide banner per activity mark (640 × 260) and a scene for the overview (`hero.svg`, 640 × 300), in the icon's style: a navy snowy night, six-armed crystals, rolling drifts in snow white to pale ice, a red jacket (and the road trip's van stripe and verge poles) as the accent and the amber glow as the only warm light. Snow country's tree is a lone conical spruce with snow on its tiers, standing on a white rise. A day card opens with its activity's banner, flush with the card's edges (`.day-art`); the overview opens with the scene above Now and Next (`.world-hero`). They're decoration (`aria-hidden`) and embedded only for the marks a trip uses. Below 600 px the scene is a strip (640 × 150, cropped to the valley's glow) and, on a trip day, a ribbon (640 × 88), so Now and Next with their buttons end on an iPhone's first screen (390 × 664) on a long trip day; the scene has no moving parts, since a crop would cut one off.

**Moving parts** ([ADR-20261121-world-motion](memory-bank/standards/decisions/ADR-20261121-world-motion.md)): a part of a drawing that moves is a group `<g data-actor="<part>" data-box="x y w h">` in the drawing itself. The build cuts each one out into a picture of its own (`engine/world-art.mjs`, carrying only the defs it uses) and the page lays it back over the rest at its box, so at rest the picture is exactly the drawing. The stylesheet moves it by `[data-actor='<motif>-<part>']`.

**Motion** (the `animate` decisions for this world):

- **One first-open snowfall:** the first time a phone opens the trip at its top, 12 flakes fall once through the Now/Next card (CSS keyframes on transform and opacity, linear, 2.4 s, staggered up to 0.6 s), then the layer is removed and never shown on that phone again (`store` key `greeted`). Under reduced motion it's never shown.
- **The candle under way flickers:** only the current stop's flame, opacity 1 ↔ 0.82 over 1.9 s and height 0.96 ↔ 1.04 from its base over 3.1 s, both alternating with `--ease-in-out`, so it never repeats on a beat. Every other candle is still, the ones in the pictures too: a flicker means "the stop under way". Under reduced motion it's still, and the stop keeps its glow and its Now flag.
- **The pictures live while on screen, each mark its own way:** each picture runs only while some of it is in view (`.is-live`, an IntersectionObserver), so a picture scrolled away stops. All of it is transform and opacity, inside the picture's own clipped box, never behind text, never moving the page. Loops swing both ways with `--ease-in-out` (constant motion is `linear`); a part that arrives eases in with `--ease-out`. Every banner has a moving part of its own (`tests/unit/worlds.test.mjs`), big enough to read on a 358 px banner.
  - The hot spring: steam rolls up off the water (a 6 % rise, height to 114 %, a 4° lean, opacity to 0.5, 4.4 s; the window's warm steam 5.6 s, leaning the other way), and rings spread across it (from 40 % to 110 %, fading out, every 3.2 s).
  - A meal: steam curls up off the bowl, quicker and twistier (a 9 % rise, a lean from 4° to −6°, 2.6 s).
  - The road trip: the van hums (2.5 % lift and 0.3° over 0.7 s), the verge poles and the near stakes stream past at two depths (one spacing per 2.6 s, linear, seamless), a puff of snow comes off the back wheel every 1.3 s.
  - The penguins waddle: 7° each way from their feet with a 3 % lift, 1.4 s, each one 0.18 s after the one ahead.
  - The rider and the skier lean through the turn: a 3.6 % lift and a 3.2° lean, 2.4 s and 2.9 s.
  - The wave's spray rises and thins (an 18 % rise, 2.4 s) and its foam slides (3.2 s).
  - The train runs along the bay, 10 s a run: it glides in from 84 % of its size, draws up where it's drawn, waits, then pulls away toward the viewer and fades out of the picture, and the next one comes.
  - The plane climbs into its place once (2.4 s, out of sight until the picture first comes into view), then flies on: it banks and lifts (6 %, 1.6°, 3.6 s) while two rows of soft clouds stream past behind it (one spacing per 12 s, linear, seamless).
  - The town: someone in a red jacket strolls off down the canal path, smaller as they go (to 42 % over 11 s, `--ease-out`, stepping ±3° every 0.55 s), fading out at the far end as the next one sets off.
  - Shopping: the shopper at the window gives two happy hops (a 9 % and a 6 % lift with a squash on landing) every 3.4 s.
  - Snow country: the lone spruce sways from its trunk (−1.4° to 1.6°, 4.6 s), powder blows off its lit side every 4.6 s, and it snows hard: bigger flakes on the wind, three tiles down and one across per fall (24 s far, 15 s near).
  - Snow falls gently in the overview, the town and shopping too: two sheets of flakes, the far one 13 s and the near one 8 s per fall, linear.
- **The sky behind the page follows the scroll** (with the sky off): nothing in it moves by itself (WCAG 2.2.2), so it never needs pausing. The sky over the views does move by itself, so it has its pause (in the header on a desktop, in More below 1024 px), stops while the page is hidden and holds still with less motion asked for.
- **The sky turns with the day:** switching views blends the band and header to the new day's weather and colour over 0.7 s (`--ease-out`); the snow changes density and speed over the same time, never by restarting.
- **Reduced motion:** every picture holds still as drawn, every part at rest and visible, no snow falls, the sky over the views is still with no pause button, and the sky behind the page is a still night with the sun risen at the foot.
- **Never:** snow falling over words, a backdrop that moves by itself without a pause, an animated glow, a filter or blur on the day cards, candles along the sides. The press scale and every default-world timing stay as they are; the week stickers press flat with the row.

**Named Rules:**

**The Nothing Hangs Rule.** Candles stand in the snow, on the ground. No hanging, floating or sky ornament in this world, not even as a mark in the week list or a sticker. The rule is about light and ornament: an illustration, the sky behind the page and the sky over the views may show what really is up there (falling snow, stars, an aurora, the sunrise, a chairlift, the plane), but never a light that hangs or floats.

**The Small Warm Light Rule.** `candle` is the only bright warm colour, and it stays in the flame and the light it throws. Never a fill, a border, text or a button. The warm day colours (Strawberry, Cocoa) are berry and earth, never amber, so nothing on a card competes with the flame; the washes are pink, mint and sky, never peach. The one other warm light is the sunrise in the closing scene, after the last word on the page.

**The Crystal Means Fixed Rule.** The ice crystal marks a fixed time and nothing else, in `seal`, the colour that means fixed in every world. So no sticker is a snowflake.

**The Sticker Rule.** A sticker is stuck on the paper, never on the words: it sits in a card's corner, its drift or a picture, takes no tap, is hidden from screen readers and never makes the page wider. It is drawn for this world (a die-cut edge and a soft shadow), never an emoji or a glyph.

## Surface: the landing page (site/)

The GitHub Pages page (`site/index.html`, `site/assets/site.css`) is the lantern-paper world applied to one page about
planning a trip: airmail postcards from the demo trip, lying under one trip day's sky
([ADR-20261002-landing-postcards](memory-bank/standards/decisions/ADR-20261002-landing-postcards.md); the direction
contract is `.impeccable/surfaces/site-index-html.md`). It copies the trip page's tokens. It starts from the system theme, and a
sun/moon button in the bar switches day and night (`theme.js`, in `<head>` so a remembered night paints from the first
frame); the browser remembers a choice only while it differs from the system's. Without the script the page follows
the system and the button stays hidden. The items below are the only deviations or extensions. Everything else follows the sections above. The design detector checks every page against this file's frontmatter, which is the trip page's, so `.impeccable/config.json` skips its design-system advisories and the airmail stripes for `site/`; the values live here and in `.impeccable/design.json` (`extensions.surfaces.site`).

- **The sky.** The ground is one day, top to bottom: `--sky-1` to `--sky-6`, a morning blue through a lilac turn
  (`--sky-35`, never grey) to an afternoon gold and the rose before dusk. A sun sits in the first screen's top right;
  clouds and birds drift over the hero and the cards. The night theme is the same page under stars (`stars.svg` at
  0.85), with clouds at 0.07 and no birds. All of it is `aria-hidden` art in `.scene`, a few small SVGs.
- **The sunset.** Where a scroll timeline exists (`@supports (animation-timeline: scroll())`), the sun keeps to the
  screen's corner and sinks as the page scrolls, fading into its low colour (`--sun-low`) by the last sections, and a
  moon rises over the footer. At night the moon drifts only 0.35 of the way. Without a scroll timeline, or under
  reduced motion, the sky stays painted down the page. Text on the bare sky keeps AA where the sun passes behind it.
- **Lanterns keep to the side lanes.** A margin lantern sits in `--lane`, the space outside the text column, so none
  is ever behind a word. Below 600 px there is no lane, so margin lanterns are hidden and only the footer's night has
  them.
- **Postcards.** Each feature is a card on postcard stock (`--stock`: white, `#161b25` at night) with 6 px corners, the
  resting `--card-shadow` and a slight tilt (−1.4° to 1.3°; ±0.4° below 700 px, where a stacked card runs about 1000 px tall and a full tilt would swing past the gutter). The back carries a 6 px band of red and blue airmail
  chevrons on its top edge, a perforated stamp (84 × 100 px, holes punched only at the edge), a round postmark at
  −11°, the brushed Chinese line (clamp 1.9rem to 2.5rem, in a deepened shade of the card's colour) and a Gabarito
  headline (800, clamp 1.5rem to 1.875rem).
- **One spot per postcard.** The picture side is one colour at full strength with a faint halftone, a light at its
  top and hills in a deeper shade: a lantern colour (1–7), the seal, airmail blue, the night, or the privacy card's
  dark room (`--vault`, `#15161a`; night grey `#343c52` in the dark theme, never inverted ink). Text on airmail blue takes `--blue-ink`, white in both themes: 5.5:1 by day, and 6.2:1 at night, where the picture sinks to `#3e60a0`. In the dark theme each
  picture sinks 40 % toward the night sky (`--pic-dusk`) and its top light turns night blue, so the cards glow like
  lanterns instead of daylight panels.
- **Phones.** An ink device frame (black at night, with a 1 px light edge): 6 px padding, 26 / 20 px radii
  (frame / screen) around the hero's real 390 px capture. A card's demo is a drawn mini trip page (`.m`: the page's
  390 × 760 layout scaled by `--k`, 0.5 to 0.64), with radii scaled from 44 px. Its chrome is the phone's: the one-row
  header over the Now stripe (`.m-hd`, `--now`) and the bottom bar (`.m-tabbar`, its items `.m-tb`), the item for the
  view on screen lit, in the day's colour on a day (`.m-tabbar.day`). The hero phone lies across its
  postcard at 9°. Don't carry these radii or the device shadow into components.
- **Pause.** Every looping demo has one: a 44 px round disc of stock under the phone's right edge, in the card's
  bottom margin, never over the screen it controls. It shows only its glyph, has an accessible name, and turns into a
  Play disc while paused.
- **Postmarks.** They're ink at 0.62 by day and a light ink at 0.82 at night, so they read on dark stock. On the
  hero's postcard the postmark sits level with the stamp, striking its left edge, and the address starts below both,
  so no line ever runs under the ink or under the phone lying across the corner.
- **Two choices in the hero.** "Plan your trip" (ink) and "Try the demo trip" (stock with a hairline edge) are the same
  size and shape, side by side, and stack full width on a phone. Nothing else competes with them there; the Google
  Maps guide waits in the second step and in the rate card.
- **The packing list and the luggage tag.** The "also packed in every page" list is a sheet the planner wrote on:
  postcard stock under the postcards' airmail edge, with a brushed line (也都在这页, "all on this page too", answering
  the hero's) in ballpoint, airmail blue mixed with ink. Each item is ticked in ballpoint, a hand-drawn airmail-blue
  stroke that turns a little from item to item, never a filled checkbox, and has a hairline in airmail blue at 16 %
  under it, never through a wrapped line. The groups, with airmail-blue headings, flow down two columns where they fit,
  so a short group leaves no hole beside a long one. The seniors facts hang beside it on a luggage tag in lantern 7
  (gold), with a punched eyelet and a string, using the trip page's key/value rows.
- **Four stamps on a route.** The how-to's four steps are stamps in lantern colours 1–4, tilted a little each way,
  with each step's brush wish set vertically, on a dashed airmail route. Each step's card is stock.
- **Rate card.** Google against the free map is a table on stock: hairline rows, a 2 px ink rule under the head and
  column subheads at label size in Ink 3. The recommended column is tinted Temple Blue (`--l2`) at 8 %, with a Temple
  Blue rule under its head, and the word "recommended" always sits beside the tint.
- **Lantern night.** The footer is the day's end: stars, a two-layer hill ridge, the moon and seven glowing lanterns,
  with light text on it. Its row of links has 44 px taps; a link inside a sentence stays inline, so its line keeps
  the paragraph's spacing.
- **Faces.** Both are self-hosted subsets with their licences in `site/assets/fonts/`: Ma Shan Zheng for the brand and

- **Motion.** The sky moves only with the scroll, never by itself. A postcard settles once, the first time it comes

  loops only while its card is in view. Under reduced motion every demo holds its still frame and the sky holds still.
  Presses keep the engine's scale to 0.97.

**The One Spot Rule.** A postcard carries one strong colour, on its picture side. Its back is stock and ink. Only the
brushed line (a deepened shade) and the stamp (a lantern of its own) carry colour. By night the colour sinks toward the
sky; it never turns into the brightest thing on the page.
